Faint Traces

a photogenic drawing you cannot draw

i. salt the paper ii. carry it into the sun iii. fix it in strong brine

Made from five random words: beautiful, draw, beside, relationship, salt.

In 1833, beside Lake Como, Henry Fox Talbot tried to draw a beautiful view and couldn't. When he lifted his eye from the camera lucida, his pencil had left “only faint traces.” So he got paper to draw for him, with salt and then silver. The image depended on the relationship between them. A little salt made the paper hungry for light. A lot of salt made it blind, so strong brine became his first fixer.

Salt flats crack into polygons a metre or two across because dense brine sinks and convects in the soil beneath the crust. The cells are about as wide as the convection is deep, and salt piles up where the flows meet (Lasser et al., Phys. Rev. X, 2023).

You can't draw the picture here either. You only lay down salt. The brine below rearranges it, the sun develops it according to the ratio, and the shadow of your hand falls beside it.
